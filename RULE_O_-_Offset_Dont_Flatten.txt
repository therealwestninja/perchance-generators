# Rule O — Offset, Don't Flatten

A design lens, not a procedure — reach for it at the forks, where Rule M (Map the Build) and Rule F
(Factory Build) start. When two things look like they should be one, or one thing needs to grow a new
capability, the reflex is to **copy** (a parallel duplicate generator/handler) or **flatten** (force
both into one uniform shape). The better move is usually a **45° rotation**: the same substrate aimed
in a new direction, so the new thing reuses the core arithmetic instead of forking it — and the
differences that were load-bearing survive. You gain a capability without a second copy to keep in
sync, and without destroying a distinction the generator relied on.

**The failure it prevents:** two near-identical generators/handlers that drift apart (fix one, the
other rots); OR a merge that looked clean and quietly deleted a real difference (light/dark stops
collapsed to one, two distinct widgets forced into one shape) and now neither reads right.

## The rotations — pick the one the situation fits

- **New surface = an ADAPTER, not a new seam.** A second output target (another preview, an export,
  a share view, a t2i prompt off the same text) reuses the shared generation through a thin adapter —
  don't rebuild the generator per surface. A new AI capability is a new **prompt/role adapter** over
  the SAME `aiTextPlugin` session, not a second plugin instance to wrangle.
- **Scalar → VECTOR.** One blunt knob (difficulty, "tone", length) → rotate it into orthogonal
  components, each a DSL list / slider the USER or the AI can turn: tone → warmth · formality ·
  verbosity. Then the thing you didn't want to build falls out free — e.g. *adaptive* tone is just a
  dial the generator turns itself by reading the chat, no separate system.
- **Decouple, then carve (two generators, one seed).** Don't make a story generator and an
  image-prompt generator (or a layout generator and a content generator) agree by MERGING them. Feed
  both the SAME seed, let each run its own way, reconcile only at the seam with a small operator —
  **carve** (cull to fit) · **filter** (gate what crosses) · **curate** (bias the mix) · **span**
  (bridge a gap) · **reconcile-to-path** (snap to a spine). One seed, two generators, one seam.
- **Generation ⇄ navigation are DUAL.** Lay-a-path and find-a-path are the same traversal from
  opposite ends: a lorebook *writer* and a lorebook *matcher*, build-a-thread and search-a-thread.
  Built one? You're most of the way to the other by rotating it, not writing it fresh.
- **Value off the win axis.** An AI character's goal need not be "answer the user." Rotate value into
  **goals-as-data** — curiosity, a mood, a secret — each with its own weight and cost, so it reads as
  an agent with its own pull, not a responder.
- **Aliveness is in LOOSENING couplings, not adding control.** A generator that feels mechanical
  rarely needs another control list — it needs one coupling deliberately loosened so a value can
  drift, with a damper to keep it in its lane. A robust generator absorbs a loose agent; a fragile one
  gets another knob and stays dead.

## When to ROTATE vs when to FLATTEN

Flatten (collapse to one shape) only **true duplicates**; rotate (keep the offset) when the
difference is **load-bearing**.
- Consolidate: 3 spellings of one color; 3 identical DSL list/`<style>` blocks; a `localStorage`
  prefix-sweep that should be an explicit key registry (also a Rule R under-capture trap).
- KEEP (give them shared TOKENS or a shared substrate, but don't fuse): gradient light/dark stops,
  depth/opacity ramps, two genuinely different widgets (a character card vs a stat row), two plugin
  seams that legitimately differ.
- The test, one line: *if I merge these, do I lose a distinction something relied on?* Yes → rotate
  (shared substrate, distinct projection). No → flatten (hoist to one source — that's refactor work).

## Anti-patterns

- **Forcing uniformity** — merging things that only look alike, deleting a real distinction.
- **Parallel copy** — forking the generator/handler per surface instead of adapting through one seam.
- **Bigger knob** — escalating a scalar instead of rotating it into a vector.
- **Merging generators** — fusing two systems to make them agree instead of decouple-then-carve.
- **Adding control to force life** — clamping tighter when the fix was a looser coupling + a damper.

## Close-out checklist

- [ ] At the fork, you asked: copy / flatten / **rotate**? and named which, and why.
- [ ] For any merge: you ran the one-line test and confirmed no load-bearing difference was lost.
- [ ] A new surface went through a thin adapter on the shared substrate — not a forked copy.
- [ ] After a decouple/reconcile, the seam was swept for wired-but-dark drift (the reverse-treeshake
      pass in Rule 0 §10) and the shape locked in a test.

## In one line

When two things want to be one, or one needs to grow — **offset, don't flatten**: reuse the substrate
at an angle (adapter · scalar→vector · decouple-then-carve · loosen-a-coupling), and keep every
difference that was load-bearing.
